# Agile + Scrum Research

## Project vs SDLC

A **project** is a temporary piece of work with a specific goal.

The **SDLC (Software Development Life Cycle)** is the process used to develop software. It usually includes planning, requirements, design, development, testing, deployment and maintenance.

In simple terms, the project is **what we are trying to achieve**, while the SDLC is **how we develop the software**.

## Agile Manifesto

The Agile Manifesto was created in **2001** by 17 software developers who met in Utah to find a better way of developing software.

It has four main values:

- Individuals and interactions over processes and tools
- Working software over documentation
- Customer collaboration over contract negotiation
- Responding to change over following a plan

## Agile Principles

The 12 principles focus mainly on:

- Delivering value to customers
- Welcoming changing requirements
- Delivering working software regularly
- Working closely with customers and developers
- Supporting motivated teams
- Good communication
- Continuous improvement
- Keeping things simple

## Different Flavours of Agile

Some common Agile approaches are:

- **Scrum**: Uses Sprints, a backlog and regular reviews.
- **Kanban**: Focuses on visualising work and improving workflow.
- **Extreme Programming (XP)**: Focuses on software quality and development practices.
- **Lean**: Focuses on delivering value while reducing waste.

## What is Scrum?

**Scrum is an Agile framework** used to manage complex work.

Teams work in short periods called **Sprints**, usually up to one month. They create a usable part of the product, get feedback and adapt their work.

## Scrum Framework

### Roles

- **Product Owner**: Manages and prioritises the Product Backlog.
- **Scrum Master**: Supports the team and helps Scrum work effectively.
- **Developers**: Create the product.

### Events

- **Sprint Planning**: Decide what to work on.
- **Daily Scrum**: Check progress and plan the next steps.
- **Sprint Review**: Review the work and get feedback.
- **Sprint Retrospective**: Discuss what went well and what could improve.

### Artifacts

- **Product Backlog**: Ordered list of work needed.
- **Sprint Backlog**: Work selected for the current Sprint.
- **Increment**: The usable product created during the Sprint.

## 3 Pillars of Scrum

The three pillars are:

1. **Transparency**: Work and progress are visible.
2. **Inspection**: Regularly check the work and progress.
3. **Adaptation**: Make changes when needed.

### In simple terms

**Agile** is a way of working, while **Scrum** is a framework for applying Agile principles.

The main idea is to **build, check, get feedback and improve continuously.**
